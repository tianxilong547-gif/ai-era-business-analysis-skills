# Write and publish a book

Turn an author’s intent and source material into a continuous, fact-bounded, reader-usable book. Treat AI as an accelerator and reviewer, never as the author’s biography, source of facts, or final rights holder.

## Select the working mode

- **New book:** begin with the reader promise and project control sheet.
- **Rescue or restructure:** preserve the old version, diagnose why it fails, then create a new version with a keep/rewrite/move/delete map.
- **Release close-out:** stop adding themes; finish content, evidence, privacy, rights, formats, rendering QA, application materials, and archive checksums.

Do not overwrite the only historical version during a major restructure.

## Start with four control documents

Create or update:

1. the project control sheet from [assets/book-project-control-template.md](assets/book-project-control-template.md);
2. the fact and rights ledger from [assets/fact-rights-ledger-template.md](assets/fact-rights-ledger-template.md);
3. the chapter contract from [assets/chapter-contract-template.md](assets/chapter-contract-template.md);
4. the revision log from [assets/revision-log-template.md](assets/revision-log-template.md).

Do not ask the author for minor anecdotes before converting confirmed material into a usable first delivery. Ask only when a missing fact or decision would materially change the book.

## Run the book pipeline

1. **Define the promise.** State the reader, real situation, usable outcome, exclusions, and minimum acceptance line.
2. **Build the evidence base.** Separate confirmed facts, unverified claims, author opinions, editorial suggestions, public sources, third-party content, and teaching constructions.
3. **Design the architecture.** Create a continuous decision or transformation path. Avoid model encyclopedias and unrelated chapter collections.
4. **Test with sample chapters.** Draft the core concept, method system, and end-to-end workflow first. Do not scale weak sample chapters into a full book.
5. **Draft the complete manuscript.** Every chapter must contain the necessary explanation and not rely on headings, figures, or templates as substitutes for prose.
6. **Integrate cases.** Prefer confirmed real cases. Mark teaching cases. Recalculate important numbers and constrain causal language to the available evidence.
7. **Restore the author.** Add confirmed judgments, lived details, rhythm, restrained humor, and language that explains why the method exists. First-person frequency alone is not authorial distinctiveness.
8. **Red-team the content.** Look for definition drift, repetition, contradicted facts, unsupported causality, privacy leaks, unusable steps, and AI overreach. Explain “red-team review” in reader language as proactive reverse questioning.
9. **Design visuals after text.** Every process must remain executable without the image. Write the purpose, nodes, arrows, branches, exact labels, privacy rules, and alt text before drawing.
10. **Produce formats.** Generate editable manuscript, review PDF, and reflowable ebook as requested. Render and inspect the actual output; source-file validity is not visual QA.
11. **Prepare release and rights.** Audit quotations, fonts, images, licenses, names, private data, AI-assisted material, publishing forms, and copyright-registration evidence.
12. **Freeze and archive.** Record version, date, file inventory, hashes, outstanding author confirmations, and the precise status: draft, review candidate, release candidate, or submitted.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- Never fabricate author experiences, employers, results, endorsements, quotations, dates, or data.
- Label confirmed facts, assumptions, unknowns, teaching constructions, and AI-assisted assets distinctly.
- Do not treat page count, file count, “no placeholder” search, or a valid PDF as content acceptance.
- Do not claim strict causality without adequate design, baseline, time window, and alternative-explanation control.
- Remove or generalize personal identifiers, internal links, account details, customer data, private metrics, and reversibly identifying combinations before public release.
- Preserve the author’s legal and commercial choices. Public GitHub access does not automatically mean an open-source license.
- Registration and publishing workflows require the author to control identity verification, signatures, contractual acceptance, payment, and final submission.

## Minimum deliverables

Scale to the user’s request, but a full-book engagement normally ends with:

- one continuous manuscript;
- final table of contents and chapter status matrix;
- fact, privacy, source, and rights ledger;
- case and cross-reference map;
- visual asset list with text equivalents and alt text;
- content review and release QA report;
- editable and review formats when requested;
- final file index, hashes, and a list of external approval gates.

State unfinished external gates honestly. “Ready to submit” and “submitted” are different statuses.
